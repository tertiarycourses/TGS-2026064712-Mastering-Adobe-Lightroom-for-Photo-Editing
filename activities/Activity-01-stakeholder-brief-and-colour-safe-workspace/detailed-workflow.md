# Activity 01 - Stakeholder Brief and Colour-Safe Workspace

**Course:** Mastering Adobe Lightroom for Photo Editing
**Course Code:** TGS-2022015367
**Alignment:** K1, K2, K3, A1, A2
**Version:** v12.0 | 19 August 2026

## Detailed Lightroom production workflow

### Before you begin

1. Work only on the supplied practice images or other images for which you have explicit permission.
2. Create an `evidence/` folder and preserve the source files unchanged.
3. Open the current Lightroom interface available to you; menu names can vary slightly across desktop, web, mobile, and Classic.
4. Save a screenshot whenever the workflow asks you to prove a setting, comparison, mask, search, permission, or export result.

### Procedure

1. Read the brief and highlight audience, destination, mood, non-negotiable colour, crop, deadline, and approval owner.
2. Ask each stakeholder two open questions; record exact words before translating them into visual criteria.
3. Convert subjective terms such as calm, premium, warm, and natural into observable acceptance statements.
4. Inspect the workspace: room light, glare, display brightness, calibration status, storage location, free space, and backup/sync state.
5. Choose Lightroom, Lightroom Classic, web, or mobile for the job and justify the choice from storage, collaboration, and delivery needs.
6. Inspect the supplied files and record format, dimensions, metadata, apparent rights source, and editing latitude.
7. Create a project folder/album and a naming convention without moving or overwriting the source files.
8. Import the selected source images, confirm that all intended files appear, and verify the original location or cloud sync state.
9. Create two versions with different creative hypotheses; change only a few variables so the comparison remains meaningful.
10. Present the alternatives against the acceptance statements and record the stakeholder decision and next action.

### Review with stakeholders

1. Present the before/after comparison and the evidence, not only the finished image.
2. Ask reviewers to respond against the brief and acceptance criteria.
3. Record the decision owner, requested change, approval status, and next action.
4. Retain rejected versions when they explain a material decision.

### Debrief questions

1. Which requirements are mandatory and which are preferences?
2. What workspace risk could cause an incorrect colour decision?
3. Why is your chosen Lightroom variant suitable?
4. What evidence shows stakeholder consultation changed the edit direction?

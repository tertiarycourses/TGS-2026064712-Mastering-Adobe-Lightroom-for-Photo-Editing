# Activity 04 - Heritage Hotel Interior: Composition, Geometry, and Local Light

**Course:** Mastering Adobe Lightroom for Photo Editing
**Course Code:** TGS-2022015367
**Alignment:** K4, K5, A2, A3
**Version:** v12.0 | 19 August 2026

## Detailed Lightroom production workflow

### Before you begin

1. Work only on the supplied practice images or other images for which you have explicit permission.
2. Create an `evidence/` folder and preserve the source files unchanged.
3. Open the current Lightroom interface available to you; menu names can vary slightly across desktop, web, mobile, and Classic.
4. Save a screenshot whenever the workflow asks you to prove a setting, comparison, mask, search, permission, or export result.

### Procedure

1. State the intended visual hierarchy and identify geometry, tonal, and distraction problems.
2. Choose the destination aspect ratio and crop for balance while preserving truthful room proportions.
3. Use Auto or Guided Upright to correct key vertical and horizontal lines; inspect stretching and edge loss.
4. Enable lens correction and remove chromatic aberration where appropriate.
5. Set global exposure and tonal endpoints before attempting local corrections.
6. Create a window/highlight mask and reduce highlights gradually; avoid grey, flat windows.
7. Create a seating-area mask using an AI selection, brush, or radial gradient and lift only enough shadow detail.
8. Refine the masks with Add/Subtract/Intersect and inspect boundaries at 100% and fit view.
9. Adjust Feather and Edge controls when available; otherwise refine with brush feather and subtract.
10. Review the edit for halos, unnatural geometry, colour contamination, and misleading spatial expansion.
11. Create a before/after and one alternative version with a different crop hypothesis.
12. Record why the selected version meets the booking-page brief better.

### Review with stakeholders

1. Present the before/after comparison and the evidence, not only the finished image.
2. Ask reviewers to respond against the brief and acceptance criteria.
3. Record the decision owner, requested change, approval status, and next action.
4. Retain rejected versions when they explain a material decision.

### Debrief questions

1. Which correction improves truth and which risks distortion?
2. Where should the eye land first?
3. How did you hide the mask transition?
4. What evidence supports the selected crop?
